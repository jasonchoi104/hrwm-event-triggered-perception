## External Description

We built a minimal runtime prototype to study event-triggered perception in dynamic environments.

In two abrupt obstacle scenarios (front-block and side-cross), a local overlap-triggered routing mechanism maintains full safety performance while reducing scene-level perception checks by up to 10–15× compared to continuous sensing.

Unlike fixed low-frequency sampling, which fails under sudden events, the system dynamically escalates perception only during actual risk exposure, resulting in demand-driven sensing behavior.

This suggests a simple but effective alternative to constant-rate perception in embodied systems, with potential applications in robotics, autonomous navigation, and edge AI.

## Core Findings

1. Reflex Router maintains safety comparable to full_scene.

2. Reflex Router significantly reduces scene-level compute proxy:

   * front_block: ~280 → ~19
   * side_cross: ~280 → ~37

3. Sensing cost scales with danger exposure duration:

   * front_block: ~15 frames → ~17 checks
   * side_cross: ~32 frames → ~37 checks

---

## Limitations

* Single obstacle scenarios only
* Heuristic trigger (distance-based)
* Scene checks used as compute proxy (not actual FLOPs)

Reflex Router sensing cost scales proportionally with actual danger exposure duration,
indicating demand-driven perception rather than fixed-rate sampling.

These limitations define the current scope of validation, rather than weaknesses of the approach.

## Insight (v0.4.x)

The experiments demonstrate that event-triggered perception routing can achieve full-scene safety performance while significantly reducing sensing overhead.

Compared to continuous full-scene sensing, Reflex Router reduces scene-level compute proxy by aligning perception frequency with actual risk exposure duration.

In abrupt obstacle scenarios, fixed low-frequency sensing fails to maintain safety, while Reflex Router preserves reliability by dynamically escalating perception only when necessary.

These results suggest that perception in embodied systems should not operate at a constant rate, but instead adapt to the temporal structure of environmental risk.
